## The core dilemma

### Your work GitHub ≠ your personal profile

Here is the fundamental hurdle with GitHub badges:

> The vast majority of software engineers write code under corporate organization accounts every day.

In practice, that means:

- Commits, pull requests, and reviews stay inside **private organizations**
- Your **personal profile** looks inactive
- Achievements stay locked — not because you are unproductive, but because of how enterprise permissions work

> 💡 **Pro tip**  
> You can occasionally negotiate with your employer to contribute using your personal GitHub handle. If you have that option, take it — most developers do not.

---

## Dishonest shortcuts

### Why gaming the system is counterproductive

There are plenty of artificial shortcuts: empty PRs, fake discussions, self-starring repos, puppet accounts.

> ⚠️ This guide focuses exclusively on **ethical, constructive, and legitimate** strategies. Every approach below is built around real contributions and genuine community interaction.

Keep in mind:

- Your contribution graph and badge history are completely **public**
- Any recruiter, engineering manager, or peer can review your activity
- Inflated or spammed profiles are easy to spot — and they hurt credibility

Insignias should showcase **actual technical competence**, not your ability to bypass platform mechanics.

> 💬 An artificially inflated profile raises immediate red flags. Spam when practical, legitimate paths exist shows poor professional judgment.

## The right way to progress

The most effective route is driving real impact through your **personal account** — ideally by building your own open-source projects.

You do not need the next groundbreaking framework. Start small:

| Idea | Why it works |
| --- | --- |
| A clean portfolio template with a colleague | Real PRs, reviews, co-authors |
| A lightweight utility package you actually use | Steady commits and incremental releases |
| Structured notes on technologies you are mastering | Discussions + documentation culture |
| A CLI helper or config boilerplate | Atomic PRs that are easy to review |

Build something functional and keep a rhythm:

1. Push commits on a regular schedule
2. Structure features as Pull Requests
3. Use Discussions for decisions and Q&A
4. Ship incremental improvements over time

Even a modest repository can showcase growth.

> ⚠️ **Security first**  
> A secure repository is a sustainable repository. Configure branch protection on primary branches, limit write access to trusted collaborators, and keep automated spam and malicious code out of the workspace.

## Understanding badge tier visualizations

### A crucial detail about how progress is displayed

> GitHub badges do **not** show your exact total count of completed actions.  
> They only reflect the **current tier or milestone** you have reached.

For example:

| What you see | What it actually means |
| --- | --- |
| `Pull Shark x2` | You unlocked the **second tier** (e.g. 16 merged PRs) — not that you only merged 2 PRs |

Knowing this removes the urge to rush or fabricate activity “just to check if it registered.”

For exact thresholds, see the [Active](README.md#active) and [Tiers](README.md#tiers) sections in the catalog.

## Quickdraw

<div align="center">
  <img src="media/badges/quickdraw.png" width="140" height="140" alt="Quickdraw">
</div>

> Close an issue or pull request within **5 minutes** of opening it.

More of a platform quirk than a technical milestone — but it still belongs on a complete showcase.

**How to earn it cleanly**

1. Open a Pull Request as part of normal work
2. Close it immediately afterward (or reopen/close during a legitimate workflow)

> ⚠️ **Avoid junk PRs.** Closing and reopening a real PR in your standard process triggers the badge just as effectively — without polluting history.

## Galaxy Brain

<div align="center">
  <img src="media/badges/galaxy-brain.png" width="140" height="140" alt="Galaxy Brain">
</div>

> Have your answer marked as the accepted solution in **GitHub Discussions**.

Rewards helpful, precise technical answers. When the thread author accepts your response, you earn progress.

Working on this inside **your own** open-source repository is valid — and it improves project documentation:

- Cleaner technical communication
- An audit trail of architectural decisions
- Better alignment on the project roadmap

> 🚀 **The common obstacle**  
> Even when you supply the exact fix, people often forget to mark an answer as accepted. Do not rely on luck — use a controlled setup with peers who have real questions.

### Approach 1 — Real queries in your network

1. Find public repositories in your tech stack with Discussions enabled
2. When a colleague hits a real problem you can solve, help them document it
3. Have them post the question and mark your answer as the official fix

✅ Creates searchable documentation for engineers facing the same issue.

### Approach 2 — Architecture debates in Discussions

If you maintain an open-source project, move technical decision-making to **GitHub Discussions**:

1. Enable Discussions in repository settings
2. Open a public thread when an architectural or feature debate arises
3. Provide structured arguments and code snippets
4. Mark the final resolution to close the thread

✅ Transparent decision log · engineering leadership · organized contributors · badge progress that looks natural.

> Note: Galaxy Brain no longer earns inside [GitHub Community](https://github.com/orgs/community/discussions/106536). **Repository** Discussions still count.

## YOLO

<div align="center">
  <img src="media/badges/yolo.png" width="140" height="140" alt="YOLO">
</div>

> Merge a self-authored Pull Request **without** requiring a review.

Recognizes agility on minor adjustments. One unlock is enough — keep it clean:

1. Apply a small, low-risk change to **your** repository:
   - A linter or formatting fix
   - A one-line docs update
   - A minor `README.md` tweak
   - The initial repository setup commit
2. Open a Pull Request
3. Merge it directly without requesting approval

> ⚠️ **Never YOLO in production.** Reserve this for your project’s first commit or minor documentation edits in a personal workspace.

## Pair Extraordinaire

<div align="center">
  <img src="media/badges/pair-extraordinaire.png" width="140" height="140" alt="Pair Extraordinaire">
</div>

> Merge a Pull Request that includes at least one **co-authored** commit.

Honors live collaboration through pair programming.

1. Partner with a collaborator on a feature or bug fix
2. Add the co-author trailer at the end of the commit message:

   ```text
   Co-authored-by: username <username@example.com>
   ```

   - `username` — exact GitHub handle  
   - `username@example.com` — email linked to their GitHub account  

3. Open the PR and merge it

One of the most respected achievements: it encourages live reviews, raises quality, and shares knowledge.

> ⚙️ **Workflow tip:** Configure a commit template in your IDE to auto-fill `Co-authored-by:` and avoid syntax errors.

> 🦈 **Synergy:** When your teammate merges the PR, they also progress toward **Pull Shark**.

Official docs: [Creating a commit with multiple authors](https://docs.github.com/en/pull-requests/committing-changes-to-your-project/creating-and-editing-commits/creating-a-commit-with-multiple-authors).

## Pull Shark

<div align="center">
  <img src="media/badges/pull-shark.png" width="140" height="140" alt="Pull Shark">
</div>

> Have your Pull Request reviewed and **merged by another contributor**.

The reliable grind: sustain development in a personal project, submit well-scoped PRs, and let collaborators review and integrate them.

⭐ Builds strong habits — clean, modular, review-friendly code.

> 💡 **Best practice:** Keep PRs atomic and small. Huge diffs slow reviews and integration.

> 🤝 **Pairing strategy:** Alternate author and reviewer roles with a teammate to progress on **Pair Extraordinaire** and **Pull Shark** together.

Tier thresholds (community-verified): see [Pull Shark tiers](README.md#pull-shark).

## Starstruck

<div align="center">
  <img src="media/badges/starstruck.png" width="140" height="140" alt="Starstruck">
</div>

> Maintain a repository that collects a significant number of **stars**.

Among the most prestigious badges — it signals genuine community validation. There are no honest shortcuts: solve a real problem and deliver tangible value.

### Two practical avenues

1. **Ship a package or utility** — identify a clear pain point and build a robust library around it  
2. **Curate a high-value resource** — a thorough technical guide, production-ready boilerplate, useful CLI, or awesome-list

### Identifying high-impact ideas

Pay attention to recurring friction:

1. **Search queries** — common setup bottlenecks across forums and communities  
2. **Framework issue trackers** — high-engagement feature requests that stay unaddressed → build a standalone package  
3. **Personal bottlenecks** — tedious config in your own workflow → turn it into a starter kit and share it  

Deliver a clean solution, write a thorough README, and put it in front of developers who feel the same pain.

Tier thresholds: see [Starstruck tiers](README.md#starstruck).
